## How the product works now (draw this, with real content)

1. **Enter a location**: drop a photo of a place (or search a place, or paste a link). ShotMatch finds where the photo was taken and builds the set: the place as a 3D world (Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles for the live view, with the Google Maps logo and data credit always visible), the solved shot camera standing where the photographer stood (Manhattan Bridge from Washington St, DUMBO; 53 mm; facing 4 degrees N; Solid, 1690 matches).
2. **The set editor**: a 3D viewport you orbit and fly through. Left: Outliner (Scene > Shot camera "Cam A · Sony a7 IV + FE 24-70 mm f/2.8 GM II @ 53 mm", Sun, Lights > "Key · area light 2x1 m 4500 K", "String lights", People > "Maya · 1.68 m · walking", "Ben · 1.80 m · standing", Props > "Vintage bicycle (generated)", "Bench", "Umbrella", Original photo) and an Asset library (People with pose thumbnails, Props, Vehicles, Lights, Cameras, "Generate a prop"). Right: Inspector for the selection (transform with numeric x/y/z in metres, rotation, scale; for a person: pose, height, facing; for a light: type, color temperature, intensity, size, shadows; for the camera: body, lens, focal, aperture, shutter, ISO, focus distance, depth of field near/far/hyperfocal, distortion profile on/off). A transform gizmo on the selected object in the viewport. Bottom: timeline for time of day (blue hour 6:05 AM and golden hour 6:35 to 7:12 AM bands, sun altitude curve, playhead), plus the saved shots as keyframes.
3. **AI on NVIDIA**: a command bar "Describe a change": "two friends walking toward camera, a red vintage bike leaning on the left wall, warm string lights overhead". Nemotron (NVIDIA's open model) turns it into scene edits and shows its steps (placed Maya at 15 m, generated bicycle with TRELLIS, added string lights). "Generate a prop" makes a 3D model from words or a photo in about 10 s (NVIDIA-hosted TRELLIS).
4. **Shot camera and render**: look through the shot camera (real lens field of view, depth of field, the lens's distortion), choose aspect and composition overlays, and press Render. The render composites your placed objects and lights over your original photo from the same camera and runs an AI render pass on NVIDIA; the result shows beside the viewport, labelled "AI interpretation". The final image is built from the user's photo and the user's objects only, never from the map tiles.
5. **Together, live**: everyone in the session edits the same set at once (SpacetimeDB): presence avatars, colored selection outlines with a name ("Maya is moving Bench"), the scout's live position as a figure in the world. The same set is reachable by iMessage: text a photo, get a link and a render back; "make it sunset" in the thread re-renders.
6. **VR** (later): put on a headset and stand on the set at 1:1 scale.
